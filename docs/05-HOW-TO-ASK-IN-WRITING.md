# How to ask your broker or insurer in writing

Why in writing: a phone call leaves nothing to point to later. An email with a dated answer does.

## A good email

- One topic. Up to six questions. Short sentences.
- Name the policy and the policy period.
- Ask for the answer in writing, and ask the broker to quote the policy wording that supports it.
- Attach your AI use list if you have one (`templates/ai-use-map.csv`).
- Say by when you would like a reply, for example 10 working days.

Ready to edit drafts: `prompts/03-BROKER-EMAIL-DRAFT.md` and `prompts/04-CARRIER-WRITTEN-REQUEST.md`. Question list: `templates/broker-question-list.md`.

## What a good answer looks like

- It names the form numbers and editions.
- It quotes the wording.
- It says plainly if something is unclear or depends on the facts of a claim.
- It is dated and comes from a named person.

## Things to be careful about

- An answer like "you are fine" with no quoted wording is not an answer to a coverage question.
- A broker's view is not the insurer's position. If the stakes are high, ask for the insurer's written position.
- Do not accuse anyone. Ask for information.
- If a problem has already happened, report it to your broker in writing right away and ask about the deadline. A request for information is not a notice of claim.
- Keep every reply with the policy. Put the date and the result in `templates/exclusion-register.csv`.

## If you do not get a reply

- Follow up after 7 to 10 working days, by email, quoting your first message.
- Ask whether someone else at the firm can answer.
- If you still get nothing, tell your broker you will record that in your register, and consider asking a licensed insurance professional for advice. This kit cannot give it.
