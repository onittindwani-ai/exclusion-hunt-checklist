# Honest limits

Read this before you trust anything in this kit.

## What this kit is not

- It is not insurance or legal advice. It does not know what coverage you have. Only your policy, read by you and your broker, and in a dispute a lawyer, can say that.
- It is for the United States only. Policies, forms and rules differ by state and by insurer.
- It does not cover every kind of policy. It looks at general liability, professional liability (errors and omissions), cyber, directors and officers (D&O), employment practices, crime and commercial property. It does not include a check for workers' compensation: none of the sources read for this kit gave AI wording for that line, so none is invented.
- It cannot tell you your policy is safe. A policy can look clean and still be disputed.

## What the sources can and cannot support

- The ISO general liability forms (CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08) are optional. Each insurer decides. The kit does not say how many insurers use them. One article quotes a forecast from a company that sells AI cover, and no source read for this kit gives a reliable count, so no number is used.
- Only two sources were read as full text: the ISO form CG 40 48 01 26 and one law firm article. Everything else was read through a page reader tool. Exact quotes from those pages are as reported. Open the page before you rely on a quote. See `03-SOURCES.md` for each source's tier.
- Several sources were found but could not be opened (Insurance Journal, Claims Journal, the American Bar Association, IRMI, Gallagher). No check rests on them. The base CGL form text (CG 00 01) was not read directly either.
- Sources disagree. Two opened sources say E&O and D&O policies carry AI exclusions from some carriers. One page says E&O, cyber and D&O have no AI exclusions. This kit reports both and tells you to read your own policy.
- Insurance wording changes. Forms are updated, and carriers change them at renewal. The information here was read on 2026-11-24 and will age.
- Some checks link to AI only by inference (check 19 on crime, check 21 on property cyber exclusions, check 26 on claims made timing). Each says so.
- Trade press, brokers and law firms write to help their readers and sometimes to sell services. Read them as views, not as facts about your policy.

## What the tools can and cannot do

- The scanner matches words. It does not understand them. A match is a place to read. No match does not prove the words are absent: copied or scanned text can be wrong, and wording can use different words for the same idea.
- The scanner and the prompts never say whether you are covered.
- The test policy in `tests/fixtures/` is made up and written in the kit's own words. It is not a real policy and not a copy of any ISO form.

## What you must still do

- Read the real policy and the forms schedule.
- Ask your broker in writing and keep the answer.
- If something has already gone wrong, tell your broker in writing now, and ask about the deadline to report it. Late notice can cost you cover.
- Get advice from a licensed professional for anything that matters.
