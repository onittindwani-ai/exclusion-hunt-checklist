# Check 20: Check whether deepfake voice or video fraud is carved out or named

- Check number: 20
- Group: Cyber, crime and property policies
- Policy types: Crime policy, cyber

## Exact words to look for

Wording from the sources (a [common] tag means ordinary wording that proves nothing alone):

- `deepfake`

Search aid words (helpers, not quotes from a form):

- `voice`
- `video`
- `manipulated`
- `impersonation`
- `payroll`
- `direct deposit`

## Where it hides in a policy

In the definition of social engineering or fraudulent communication, and in endorsements that renew each January.

## Why it matters

A law firm article says narrow definitions of social engineering may exclude voice deepfakes or manipulated video, or transactions such as payroll direct deposit changes. A trade source reports that some carriers are removing deepfake fraud from social engineering cover at January renewals, while others offer it as an affirmative extension. Read the definition of the trick, not just the headline of the cover.

## What you do

1. Read the definition of social engineering or fraudulent communication.
2. Check whether the words voice, video or deepfake appear, and in which direction.
3. Ask your broker to confirm in writing whether a fake voice call is covered.

## Ask your broker or carrier

> Does my definition of social engineering fraud include fake voice calls, fake video and fake images of a real person? Please quote the words.

Send it by email so the answer is in writing.
