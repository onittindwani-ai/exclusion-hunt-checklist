- `voluntary parting`

## Where it hides in a policy

In the crime policy as an endorsement often titled social engineering fraud, with a schedule showing the cap and conditions. Also check the cyber policy.

## Why it matters

Social engineering is fraud that tricks a person into sending money, such as a fake email from a boss or supplier. Law firm commentary says the standard crime coverages (computer fraud, funds transfer fraud, employee theft) are often argued not to fit a payment you were tricked into making, so insurers add a social engineering endorsement. It reports caps commonly between 25,000 and 250,000 dollars and conditions such as call back checks. A trade article about personal scams says most crime and cyber policies bar cover when you authorize a transfer even though you were deceived, a clause some call voluntary parting (that article is about personal lines, so treat the term as secondary). AI makes fake messages and voices easier to produce.

## What you do

1. Find the social engineering endorsement. Write down the cap and the conditions.
2. Check you can really follow the conditions, such as calling back a known number before paying.
3. Ask your broker whether cap and conditions fit the size of payments you make.

## Ask your broker or carrier

> Do my crime and cyber policies cover payments I was tricked into making? What is the cap and what must we do before paying for cover to apply?

Send it by email so the answer is in writing.
