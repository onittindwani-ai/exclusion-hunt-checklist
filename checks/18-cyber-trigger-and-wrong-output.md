# Check 18: Check whether cyber cover needs a breach, or also responds to wrong AI output

- Check number: 18
- Group: Cyber, crime and property policies
- Policy types: Cyber

## Exact words to look for

Wording from the sources (a [common] tag means ordinary wording that proves nothing alone):

- `unauthorized access` [common]

Search aid words (helpers, not quotes from a form):

- `security breach`
- `network security`
- `data breach`
- `computer system`
- `security event`

## Where it hides in a policy

In the definitions of a cyber policy: how it describes the event that triggers cover (for example a breach or unauthorized access to a computer system).

## Why it matters

Cyber cover is usually triggered by a security event. One person quoted in trade press says harmful AI output without an outside breach generally falls outside cyber, because nobody has breached you. A law firm article says cyber policies were not designed to address all AI risks, in particular liability from AI outputs. A Big I trade article says cyber insurers are not broadly excluding AI risks. These views differ, so ask. The first is a view as reported, not a rule.

## What you do

1. Find the definition of the event that triggers cyber cover.
2. Compare it with a case where AI sends a wrong email or gives a wrong answer with no outside attack.
3. Ask your broker in writing which policy would respond to that case.

## Ask your broker or carrier

> If an AI tool sends wrong information to a customer and nobody has hacked us, which of my policies responds? Please point to the wording.

Send it by email so the answer is in writing.
