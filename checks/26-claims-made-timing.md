# Check 26: Check claims made timing: retroactive date, extended reporting period and which year's policy responds

- Check number: 26
- Group: Timing, filings and silence
- Policy types: Professional liability, D&O, cyber and any claims made policy

## Exact words to look for

Wording from the sources (a [common] tag means ordinary wording that proves nothing alone):

- `retroactive date` [common]
- `extended reporting period` [common]

Search aid words (helpers, not quotes from a form):

- `claims-made`
- `claims made`
- `notice of circumstances`
- `reported`

## Where it hides in a policy

On the declarations page (retroactive date, policy period) and in the claims made conditions and extended reporting endorsements.

## Why it matters

A claims made policy covers claims reported while the policy is active, for events after its retroactive date. An extended reporting period, sometimes called a tail, gives extra time to report after the policy ends. AI mistakes can show up long after the work was done, so dates matter. A law firm blog also advises reviewing earlier policies that came before AI exclusions, because they may offer broader cover for a current claim.

## What you do

1. Write down the retroactive date and policy period of each claims made policy.
2. Keep every old policy. Never throw one away.
3. If you find an AI mistake, tell your broker in writing right away, even if no claim has been made.

## Ask your broker or carrier

> What are the retroactive date and extended reporting terms on my claims made policies, and by when must I report a possible problem?

Send it by email so the answer is in writing.
